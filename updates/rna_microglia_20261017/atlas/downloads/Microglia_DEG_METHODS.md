# Microglia RNA gene differential expression, 25 October 2026

- Source: 22,251 provisional Microglia-like RNA cells from the accepted GSE212606 atlas, across 20 mice and 192 technical libraries. The saved Leiden 1.8 embedding assigns 24 provisional Microglia subclusters. The new mouse-by-subcluster raw integer UMI sums exactly reproduce the previously saved 20-mouse total gene-count matrix for all 55,487 features.
- Model: edgeR robust quasi-likelihood on raw integer mouse pseudobulks with TMM library normalization and a design containing sex and the five conditions. Each target is compared to WT_3mo. Feature filtering uses `filterByExpr`. The FDR is calculated within each population and contrast; no test treats cells as biological replicates.
- Subcluster eligibility: a mouse contributes only if it has at least 10 cells in the subcluster and positive gene depth. A contrast is tested only with at least three eligible mice in both control and target groups and a full-rank sex-adjusted design. Dashes in the heatmap denote untested contrasts. Subcluster identities remain provisional; differences in cell composition can affect pseudobulk results.
- Association: FDR < 0.1 DEGs are cross-referenced to TE-overlapping ATAC peaks within 100 kb of the gene TSS that have target/control group accessibility log2 change at least 0.5. Up to five closest peaks are retained per DEG and population. One peak can list several RepeatMasker subfamilies, each represented separately. These are genomic proximity and group-level concordance candidates, not causal peak-to-gene links. TE RNA subfamily log2FC and FDR are shown separately; no disease contrast has a TE RNA subfamily at FDR < 0.1.
- Colors: low `#3288bd`, high `#d53e4f`; the live atlas permits palette and point-size changes. Original publication-control plots retain their source-oriented colors.
- Outputs: all tested DEG tables, support and summary tables, association candidates, PDF/PNG figures, exact scripts and software-session information are in the downloadable archive. The browser payload is a compact visualization subset; use the full tables for analysis.
